Every change in qbrix has a person behind it. People act in the console, or through a **connection**: their Claude Code, Cursor or own script, acting for them. Apps that only ask for decisions and report outcomes use keys, which can never change anything.

## Who acts in qbrix

| Actor | What it is | How it signs in | Shown in history as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Member** | A person in the workspace, with a role: admin, member or viewer | The console | Mustafa |
| **Connection** | A client acting for one member | OAuth, started from the client | Mustafa via Claude Code |
| **Key** | What an app uses to ask for decisions and report outcomes | A key in the SDK | Not in history: a key can't make changes |

A connection can never do more than its person. What it may do is the person's role, checked on every call, so a viewer's connection can only read, and changing someone's role changes what their connections may do from their next call.

## Changes that ask first

For each kind of change, a connection has one of three levels:

- **Act on its own:** the change applies at once.
- **Act and notify:** the change applies at once, and you are told.
- **Ask first:** the change waits until you approve it.

A new connection asks first to launch a loop, start a cycle or retire a loop, because these put something new in front of users or can't be fully undone. It creates drafts, metrics and notes on its own, and acts and notifies for everything else. Undoing a change runs at the level of the change it undoes. Reads have no level.

A change that asks first is stored, waiting, with the client's reason and what it would change, and nothing applies. You answer it in your client, when it can ask you through [MCP](/docs/mcp-server#changes), or in your inbox in qbrix. Only you answer your connections' changes, and only if your role could make the change yourself:

- **Approve:** the change applies, and history records that you approved it and where, for example "approved by Mustafa in Claude Code" or "approved by Mustafa in qbrix". You can also let the connection act and notify for that kind from now on; that is recorded as a change of its own.
- **Decline:** the change stays in history with your reason, if you give one. Agents read a loop's declined changes before proposing again.

Your inbox holds what your connections asked for, oldest first, each with what it would change and how often that connection asked for the same kind lately and you approved; the changes your connections made at act and notify in the last seven days, with undo; and every loop qbrix paused that no one has resumed, which every member of the workspace sees. Nothing waits on a viewer, so a viewer's inbox holds alerts only. Nobody else sees your waiting changes, admins included. The count beside Inbox in the navigation is what waits on you. The same read is `GET /api/v1/inbox`, and the count is `GET /api/v1/inbox/count`.

A waiting change never expires, and one change of each kind waits on a loop at a time. When you approve, it runs against the loop as it is then. If the loop has moved so that the change no longer applies, for example the loop was committed while a new cycle waited, nothing applies, qbrix names the change that moved it, and the change keeps waiting until you decline it. A change in between that doesn't stop it, such as a rename, doesn't block it.

## Signing a client in

You add qbrix to your client with one address, the [MCP server](/docs/mcp-server). The first time the client calls qbrix, it finds qbrix's sign-in by itself and opens your browser:

1. You sign in to the console, if you aren't already.
2. qbrix shows the client's name, where it comes from, and the [autonomy](#changes-that-ask-first) it starts with.
3. You approve, and the client receives its access. You can decline instead, and the client gets nothing.

Nobody registers an app first: clients identify themselves, either by publishing a description of themselves at a URL or by registering when they first sign in. The access a client receives works only for your qbrix's MCP server, never for the API or anything else.

There is one connection per client per person. Signing in again from the same client renews that connection instead of adding a second one, and the access it had before stops working.

## How long a connection lasts

- **Access lasts 8 hours**, then the client refreshes it without asking you. Each refresh replaces the previous one.
- **A connection lasts until it is revoked, or until it goes unused for 90 days.** Then you sign in again from the client. There is no maximum lifetime.
- **A refresh can be used once.** If one is presented a second time, one of the two holders isn't your client, so qbrix revokes the connection, and you sign in again.

## Seeing and revoking connections

Everyone sees their own connections, with the client's name, when it was connected and when it was last used. An admin sees every connection in the workspace.

You can revoke any of your connections, and an admin can revoke any in the workspace. Revoking one stops it on its next call. It doesn't sign you out of the console, and your other connections keep working.

Each connection also has its own request limit: 120 calls a minute, of which at most 30 changes. A call over MCP reads loops, history and results, which costs far more than a decision, so the limit sits well below the API's. One misbehaving client can't use up anyone else's calls or slow anyone's decisions, and a client that loops on changes is stopped within a minute. Reads keep working when changes run out.

## On your own install

Signing a client in uses OAuth 2.1, which requires HTTPS everywhere except `localhost`. Before you connect clients from other machines:

- Serve qbrix over HTTPS, and set `CONSOLE_URL` to that `https://` address. qbrix names itself to clients by this address.
- If the API isn't on the console's address, set `PROXY_PUBLIC_URL` to the address clients reach the API at.
- Route `/oauth`, `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`, `/mcp` and `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource` to the API, as `/api` is. The Docker Compose gateway and the Helm chart already do. See [self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting).

A client describes itself at a URL on the public internet, which qbrix fetches when the client signs in. An install that can't reach the internet still works: the client registers itself instead.

Asking in your client relies on the API's secret box key, `PROXY_SECRET_BOX_KEY` (`SECRET_BOX_KEY` in the Docker Compose `.env`). `bin/selfhost-init` and the Helm chart generate it; an install from before this release adds a long random value by hand, and the API won't start with qbrix next on without one. Never change it once set.

To change a connection's limits, set `PROXY_MCP_CALLS_PER_MINUTE` (default `120`) and `PROXY_MCP_CHANGES_PER_MINUTE` (default `30`). Both must be at least `1`, and the API won't start otherwise. The cap on changes only binds below the cap on calls. With the Helm chart, pass them through `proxy.extraEnv`.
